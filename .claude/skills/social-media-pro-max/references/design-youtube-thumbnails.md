# YouTube Thumbnail Design

The thumbnail + title together gate everything: **CTR × average view duration = reach**. A great video with a weak thumbnail dies. Aim for CTR 4–10%+ (varies by niche/traffic source).

## Specs
- **Resolution**: 1280×720 (16:9), min 640px wide. Under 2MB. JPG/PNG.
- Must look great at **tiny size** (mobile feed ≈ 250px wide) AND large. Design at small scale first.
- Renders on light AND dark UI; sits beside competitors' thumbnails — must out-contrast them.

## The core principle: ONE idea, instantly
A viewer decides in ~0.5s. The thumbnail must convey a single, emotionally charged idea at a glance.

## Anatomy of a high-CTR thumbnail
1. **Focal subject** — usually an expressive human face (strong, genuine emotion: shock, joy, curiosity, tension). Faces + eye contact are the strongest CTR drivers. Big, well-lit, foreground.
2. **Text** — **3–6 words MAX** (often 1–3). Huge, bold, high-contrast. NOT the title repeated — it adds a new angle, curiosity, or stakes ("$0 → $10k", "I WAS WRONG", "DON'T DO THIS").
3. **Contrast & focal pop** — subject separated from background via outline/stroke, drop shadow, blur, or color contrast. Bright/complementary colors (orange/teal, yellow/blue, red/black). Avoid muddy mid-tones.
4. **Curiosity gap / emotion** — show the stakes or the "what?!" moment. Arrows, circles, before/after splits, X marks, glow direct the eye and raise tension.
5. **Composition** — clear foreground/background separation, rule of thirds, breathing room. Don't crowd. Keep critical elements out of the **bottom-right** (timestamp overlay).

## Title ↔ thumbnail synergy
- They should **complement, not duplicate**. Title says one thing; thumbnail shows/adds another → bigger curiosity gap.
- Together they make a promise the video must pay off (false promise = low retention = throttled reach).

## Style system
- **Fonts**: 1, occasionally 2. Heavy/black weight sans (Anton, Montserrat Black, Bebas, Archivo Black). Add thick stroke/shadow so text survives on any background.
- **Color**: a saturated, branded palette; high contrast; consistent across your channel for recognition.
- **Brand consistency**: same color accents, font, treatment, and (often) the same face framing across videos = recognizable in the feed.
- **Quality**: sharp, well-lit, high-res. No clutter, no tiny text, no low-res screenshots.

## Process
1. Decide the ONE emotion/idea before designing.
2. Sketch 3 concepts; pick the strongest contrast + clearest focal point.
3. Cut text to the fewest possible words.
4. **Shrink to 250px and squint** — is the subject + message instantly clear? If not, simplify.
5. Compare against the actual competing thumbnails in that search/feed — does yours pop?
6. A/B test (YouTube's thumbnail test feature) when available.

## Output spec template
```
Size: 1280×720 (16:9)
Big idea (one emotion): <e.g., "shock at a result">
Focal subject: <face / object>, foreground, lit, with stroke/separation
Text: "<3–6 words, new angle vs title>"  — font [Anton/Montserrat Black], white w/ black stroke
Title (companion): "<title that complements, not repeats>"
Palette: bg #__, subject pop #__, text #__ (high contrast)
Eye-direction devices: [arrow/circle/before-after?]
Keep clear of bottom-right (timestamp). Test at 250px.
```

## Anti-patterns
- Title text repeated verbatim on the thumbnail.
- Paragraphs / >6 words / tiny fonts.
- Low contrast, muddy colors, dark-on-dark.
- Cluttered, multiple competing focal points.
- Misleading clickbait the video doesn't deliver (kills retention → reach).
- Important elements in the bottom-right corner.
