# Writing for Substack & Newsletters — Growth Playbook

## What drives growth (2025–2026)
- **Open rate** (subject line + sender trust) and **read-through** (the writing itself).
- **The Substack network**: Recommendations, Notes, and being shared/restacked are the biggest free-growth levers. Treat Notes like a native social feed (it behaves like early Twitter).
- **Forwards and "share" clicks** — write things people send to one person.
- **Consistency** — a fixed cadence trains both the algorithm and the reader's habit.
- Free → paid conversion comes from trust + a clear reason to upgrade (depth, archive, community, extras).

## The subject line (your real headline)
The single biggest lever on opens. Rules:
- 4–9 words, concrete, curiosity or clear benefit. Front-load the interesting word.
- Avoid spammy caps/exclamation and clickbait you don't pay off.
- Patterns: "The one thing about X nobody tells you", "Why I stopped [common practice]", "How [person] did [result]", "[Number] [things] that changed how I [verb]".
- A/B mentally: would *you* open it in a crowded inbox?

## Preview text (second hook)
The snippet after the subject. Don't waste it on "View in browser". Add a second curiosity beat or the promise.

## The essay structure
1. **Open with a hook** — a scene, a confession, a sharp claim, or a question. No throat-clearing ("In this post I will…").
2. **The promise** — within the first ~100 words the reader knows what they'll get.
3. **One core argument/story**, developed with specifics, examples, and your distinct voice. Subheads + short paragraphs for scannability.
4. **Texture** — anecdotes, data, screenshots, pull-quotes. Vary rhythm; short punchy lines after long ones.
5. **Landing** — a resonant close that ties back to the open. Leave them with a line worth quoting.
6. **CTA** — subscribe, comment (ask a real question), share, or upgrade — pick ONE primary ask.

Length: 800–2,500 words. Depth and a singular voice matter more than length; respect the reader's time.

## Voice & positioning
- A newsletter is a **relationship**. Write to one person. First person, opinionated, generous.
- Develop a recognizable voice and a clear "beat" (what you're the person for).
- Vulnerability + insight is the most shared shape.
- Be the signal in a noisy inbox: cut filler, no fluff intros.

## Growth engine
- **Notes**: post 1–3×/day — short ideas, excerpts, hot takes, behind-the-scenes. This is your top-of-funnel; restacks bring new subscribers.
- **Recommendations**: exchange with aligned writers; this is the strongest compounding loop on the platform.
- **Repurpose inbound**: turn your best tweets/threads/LinkedIn posts into Notes and vice versa.
- **Lead magnet**: a strong welcome email + a reason to subscribe on every public post.
- **Cross-post the hook** to X/LinkedIn with a link to the full piece.
- Ask happy readers to forward; add a one-line "forward this to someone who…" near the end.

## Free vs. Paid
- Give the free tier real value (build trust). Gate depth, archives, full series, community, or extras behind paid.
- Make the upgrade ask specific and recurring but not desperate. Annual + founding tiers raise LTV.

## Anti-patterns
- Weak/clever-but-vague subject lines that hurt opens.
- Burying the lede under a long personal preamble.
- Inconsistent cadence (the #1 killer of newsletters).
- Walls of text with no subheads on mobile.
- Treating Notes as a broadcast-only channel instead of engaging.
